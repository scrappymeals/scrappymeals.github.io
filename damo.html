How I Solve Problems

Data tells me what's happening. Creative reasoning tells me why, and what to do about it.

I don't treat analysis and creativity as separate modes. The metrics tell me where the problem lives; the pattern-matching that comes from a career in live broadcast, animation, and product design tells me what to actually try — and a dashboard rarely gets there alone.

  • Own KPI definition, subscriber and engagement forecasting, and product-health metrics end to end — not just reporting on them after the fact.
  • Build advanced spreadsheet and SQL-level models to pressure-test a hypothesis before it becomes a roadmap bet.
  • Partner directly with data science and analytics teams to turn "what does the data say" into "what should we build."
  • Treat every dashboard as the start of a question, not a finished answer — the interesting move is usually the one the numbers alone can't justify yet.

Operating Model

Build the engine, not just the artifact.

Every project runs through the same loop, regardless of domain: define what we're actually trying to move, decide what success looks like before building anything, look at the real data, find the actual problem, generate options, pick the highest-leverage one, commit, ship, measure, and adjust.

01

Define the Goal

Start from the business or user outcome the work needs to move — not the deliverable, the underlying goal it serves.

02

Define Success

Translate the goal into a specific, falsifiable success metric before any building starts, so "done" has a real definition.

03

Observe the Metrics

Pull the baseline data and existing signals to understand what's actually happening before changing anything.

04

Identify the Problem

Diagnose root cause from the metrics and context, not just the visible symptom or the loudest complaint.

05

Brainstorm Ideas

Generate a wide set of possible interventions before narrowing, so the first idea isn't mistaken for the best one.

06

Prioritize for Efficiency

Rank options by expected impact against cost, effort, and time to find the highest-leverage move, not the easiest one.

07

Decide & Commit

Make the call with the information available and move — decisive, confident execution beats endless analysis.

08

Ship & Measure

Implement, instrument, and observe what actually happened against the success metric defined up front.

09

Learn & Adjust

Iterate on real results — small, deliberate adjustments compounded over time, not sweeping overcorrections.
