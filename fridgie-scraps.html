Character System

Click either card to open their recurring behaviors. Scraps creates emotional energy — Fridgie gives that energy direction. Neither character speaks a language.

Voice & language model

No character dialogue, in any language. A single off-screen narrator carries the story ("Scraps couldn't resist just one more bite…"), while Fridgie and Scraps communicate entirely through sound, gesture, and expression — closer to Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner than to a talking-animal sitcom. This makes the show translation-proof: swapping markets means recording one new narrator track, not re-casting and re-timing two characters' dialogue.

Emotional engine · no dialogue

Scraps

Curious, impulsive, hungry, expressive, physical, occasionally overconfident. Vocalizes like a mischievous little gremlin — babble, gasps, squeaks, hums — never real words.
Vocal identity Minion-style gibberish and non-verbal sounds carry emotion in place of lines; pitch and rhythm do the acting. Physical comedy Dramatic sniffing; hiding food; rushing; dancing badly; celebrating too early. Food behavior Hesitates, investigates, grabs too much, changes his mind, discovers one small thing.

Possibility engine · no dialogue

Fridgie

Warm, clever, playful, calm under pressure. Speaks through light patterns, chimes, and gesture — the narrator translates intent, Fridgie never lectures in words.
Signals Door-glow pulses, chime tones, and door-swing gestures stand in for offering choices, giving a hint, or flagging a safety beat. Relationship rule

Play it like a chase-partnership, not a conversation: Scraps creates the problem through action, Fridgie complicates or redirects it through a visual gag or signal, and the narrator stitches the beats together the way a storybook narrator would over silent slapstick. Either one can solve the problem, they celebrate together, and no scene should ever depend on one character verbally explaining something to the other.
