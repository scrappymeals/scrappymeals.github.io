Together

User enters chicken, half an onion, rice, cheddar → Fridgie offers three real recipes → Scraps drops a challenge ("rescue the rice before midnight") → user cooks → app logs ingredients used, leftovers rescued, money saved, streak extended. That's a full product loop, not a one-off AI reply.

The Rescue Mission, step by step

This is the atomic loop everything else plugs into. Tap a step for detail.

1

🥕 Tell Fridgie what you have

Fast ingredient entry — type or speak. No planning, no shopping list. Whatever's actually in the fridge right now.

2

🧊 Fridgie generates real options

Three practical recipes, tuned to equipment, diet, and household size — not a generic AI wall of text.

3

🍬 Scraps issues the challenge

"Rescue the rice before midnight"

Turns the recipe choice into a game, not a chore.

4

🍳 User cooks, taps "I Made This"

Confirms completion — the moment the app learns what actually got cooked, not just browsed.

5

🧾 Rescue Receipt + optional Remix

Ingredients used, leftovers rescued, estimated money saved, streak extended, recipe saved to Cookbook — and a shareable card is born.

Recipe Remix, upgraded

Not just "regenerate another recipe" — a structured, reusable content object. This is the dataset generic recipe sites don't have: what people actually made from incomplete, real-life ingredients.

A Remix contains

Original ingredients · Fridgie's generated recipe · user substitutions · equipment used · dietary adaptation · prep time · photo or short video · rating · "would I make this again?" · ingredients rescued · estimated cost · optional story · attribution to the original Remix.

The Fridgie Kitchen Brain

Not "switching costs" as the pitch — the real benefit is that Fridgie progressively understands your actual kitchen. The key distinction: what you cooked, not just what you saved.

Household

People & ages, serving sizes, allergies & dietary restrictions, disliked ingredients, cuisine preference, budget sensitivity, typical cook time.

Kitchen

Oven, air fryer, microwave, blender, slow cooker, grill, pans & tools on hand, comfort level in the kitchen.

Behavior

Frequent ingredients, common leftovers, saved vs. actually cooked recipes, preferred substitutions, weeknight vs. weekend patterns.
